Security monitoring

Every sensor reading is checked for signs of a cyberattack. Use the panel on the right to launch a simulated attack and watch it being detected.

How attacks are detected
Physics rules
Checks that readings obey physics, e.g. a room cannot warm up by 4 °C in five minutes, and CO₂ must rise when people are present.
ML anomaly detector
An Isolation Forest learned what normal readings look like for each zone and flags readings that are very unlike them.
Drift test
Compares the last 6 hours of readings with the training data, to catch changes too slow for the other two checks.

An alert is only a suspicion. The AI agent then re-checks it (see the AI Agent page).

Slow changes over time (drift)

Each dot is a 6-hour window of readings from one zone that looked clearly different from the data the detectors were trained on. Hover over a dot for details.

◆ Diamonds: security drift (possible attack). Relationships that should never change have shifted, e.g. CO₂ no longer matches the number of people. This can mean someone is manipulating the sensors slowly.
● Circles: data drift (not an attack). Ordinary values (temperature, humidity, CO₂, power) differ from the training period, usually because of the season or a change in usage. The ML model may get less accurate and should be retrained.

The colour shows which measurement changed.

PSI (height of the dot) measures how much the spread of values changed:

  • below 0.1: no real change
  • 0.1–0.25: small change
  • above 0.25 (red dashed line): significant change

KS test p-value is the chance that a difference this large is pure luck. A tiny value such as 5e-17 (0.000…005) means the change is certainly real.
